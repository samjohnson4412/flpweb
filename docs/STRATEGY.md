# Strategic read

Conclusions drawn from the gala script, the sponsorship tracker, the auction
fulfilment sheet, Givebutter results, and the Facebook history. These go beyond
website scope, but several of them *are* website problems in disguise.

---

## 1. The website is costing you sponsorship money, measurably

The sponsorship tracker records ~320 companies approached, **560 applications
submitted, and 47 donations** — roughly **8% conversion**. The recorded reasons
for rejection are the interesting part. They are overwhelmingly *procedural*, not
"we don't care about disaster relief":

> "Can't verify EIN" · "Needs State Tax Exempt" · "Form isn't for 501c3" ·
> "No Pass Through Non-Profits" · "Must know an employee" · "Need 90 days"

Corporate giving officers verify a nonprofit before approving anything, and the
first place they look is the website. Here is what yours currently shows them:

| They check for | Your site has |
| --- | --- |
| Legal name matching the EIN | **Wrong** — footer still says Sylvan Ramble Lights, Inc. |
| State charitable registration | **Missing** — disclosure has no CH number |
| Evidence of program work | **"Our Impact — Coming Soon!"** |
| Financials / Form 990 | **Absent** |
| Board & governance | 4 names, no roles beyond title, **no treasurer** |
| Recent activity | Newest content is a concluded campaign |

A reviewer landing on that page cannot confirm you are what you say you are. At
least four of the recorded rejections — EIN verification, tax-exempt status,
501c3 form eligibility, pass-through concerns — are questions a properly built
**Transparency page** answers before anyone has to ask.

**This is the highest-ROI page on the new site, and it isn't in the current IA.**
Add `/about/transparency`: legal name, EIN, state registration number,
determination letter, Form 990s, board roster with roles, annual impact numbers.

Related: **you currently have no treasurer.** That's a governance gap a diligent
corporate reviewer or grantmaker will notice, and it sits directly upstream of
the "can't verify" rejections. Worth filling before the next sponsorship cycle.

## 2. The gala probably lost money

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Budgeted cost (business plan) | $9,000 |
| Raised (Givebutter) | $7,259 |
| Goal | $50,000 |
| Attendance | 49 supporters |
| **Net, before labor** | **≈ −$1,700** |

Seven courses, five custom videos, an emcee script, a mayor's endorsement, a
venue, and a volunteer corps — to finish **14% to goal and underwater**.

The gala is the most labor-intensive thing the organization does and its worst
performer. Compare:

| Channel | Result | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light show (2015–2023) | ~$50,000 · 20,000+ visitors · **NBC Nightly News** | Volunteer, own property |
| Facebook: Hilton rooms offer | **700 shares**, organic | $0 |
| Beacons of Hope gala 2025 | $7,259 from 49 people | $9,000 |

The pattern is unambiguous: **your winning channels are mass-participation and
concrete-offer-driven. Your losing channel is high-touch and high-production.**

This argues strongly for reviving the light show as "Lanterns of Hope" — not for
nostalgia, but because it is the only thing you've ever done that produced both
money *and* national earned media, at near-zero marginal cost. It also reaches
thousands of people rather than 49.

Not a recommendation to kill the gala. It builds relationships, and the videos it
produced are your best assets. But it should be sized as a **cultivation event
that breaks even**, with fundraising pressure moved to recurring giving.

## 3. The auction destroyed 72% of the value donated to it

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Retail value donated | $11,699.73 |
| Actually realized | $3,319.00 |
| **Realization rate** | **28.4%** |
| Unsold items | 7 ($856 retail) |
| Unique bidders | **22** |
| Top 5 bidders' share | **64%** of auction revenue |

Forty-eight businesses were solicited, tracked, thanked, and given a page on the
website — to convert $11,700 of donated goods into $3,319, split among 22 people.

**The clearest signal in the data:** items with obvious cash-equivalent value
cleared at or above retail, while bundled "experiences" collapsed.

| Beat retail | Collapsed |
| --- | --- |
| Ocean Prime $200 → **$220** | Portrait + Miami hotel $5,000 → **$150** (3%) |
| Publix $100 → **$105** | Let's Roam group of 10 $150 → **$20** (13%) |
| 1905 Restaurants $100 → **$105** | Wine tasting for 20 $600 → **$130** (22%) |
| Brew Bus $80 → **$184** | MOSI 6-month for 5 $125 → **$35** (28%) |

With 22 bidders in the room, nobody needs a wine tasting for 20 people. The
inventory was mismatched to the audience size — a fixable problem, but it means
**stop soliciting large-group experiences for a small gala.**

That $5,000 portrait session selling for $150 also carries a warning for the
website: **don't publish inflated retail values on sponsor pages.** A stated
value the market rejects by 97% invites skepticism about every other number you
publish.

## 4. There is an unresolved fulfilment problem

From the auction fulfilment sheet:

| Status | Items | Value |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Confirmed Yes | 11 | $473 |
| **"Probobly"** | **32** | **$2,536** |
| **No** | **4** | **$310** |

**36 of 47 sold items have no confirmed delivery.** People paid for those. Four
are recorded as flatly not fulfilled.

This is the single most urgent item in any of these documents, and it has nothing
to do with the website. Winners who never received what they paid for are exactly
the people least likely to attend next year — and most likely to say so publicly.
A $310 problem becomes a reputation problem at a 49-person event where everyone
knows each other.

Worth closing out before launch, because a new site will bring renewed attention.

## 5. Your own services were market-tested, and the market said no

Two FLP earned-revenue services were listed in the auction:

| Service | Listed | Sold | Realization |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Professional Weather Station + Install | $650 | $280 | 43% |
| Pre-Storm Drone Property Inspection | $250 | $80 | 32% |

Both underperformed gift cards, which cleared ~100%. Against a warm room of
supporters who already believe in the mission.

That's real pricing signal. Either the prices are above what the market bears, or
**the value isn't legible** — nobody in that room knew what a pre-storm drone
inspection gets them. The business plan leans on these as sustaining revenue
(Lantern Network Membership, drone inspections, weather station services), so this
matters.

For the website: these can't be line items on a services list. They need pages
that do the explaining the auction table couldn't — what you get, what it's worth,
what it costs, and **Maria's story**, which is the proof that an inspection report
turns into an SBA loan. That story converts the abstract into arithmetic.

## 6. Recurring giving is the only thing that fixes the underlying problem

Every revenue source above is episodic: a gala, a campaign, a storm. Disaster
relief demands the opposite — money in hand *before* the storm, so the answer can
be yes within hours. The gala script says this out loud:

> Every monthly gift we secure tonight gives us the ability to say "yes" with
> confidence.

That's why **Lantern Keeper** is now the site's primary CTA instead of a concluded
Jamaica campaign. It's also the only ask where the website does the whole job —
no event, no venue, no volunteers, no fulfilment.

**OPEN:** how many Lantern Keepers did the gala actually produce? That number
determines how hard the entire site should push it.

## 7. What this means for the build

Reordered by expected return:

1. **`/about/transparency`** — unblocks sponsorship conversions. Highest ROI page.
2. **`/give/lantern-keeper`** — the only fully self-serve revenue channel.
3. **`/response/*`** — Helene and Milton written up permanently. This is what
   grantmakers ask for, and the "one major grant" goal depends on it.
4. **Service pages carrying Maria's and Peter's stories** — make the earned-
   revenue offers legible enough to price correctly.
5. **`/events/lanterns-of-hope`** — the light show is the proven channel.
6. Sponsor pages, blog, everything else.

Note that the current site's ordering is almost exactly inverted: ~96 of its 109
pages are sponsor detail pages.
