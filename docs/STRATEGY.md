# Strategic read

Conclusions drawn from the gala script, the sponsorship tracker, the auction
fulfilment sheet, Givebutter results, and the Facebook history. These go beyond
website scope, but several of them *are* website problems in disguise.

---

## 1. The website is costing you sponsorship money, measurably

The sponsorship tracker records ~320 companies approached, **560 applications
submitted, and 47 donations** — roughly **8% conversion**. The recorded reasons
for rejection are the interesting part. They are overwhelmingly *procedural*, not
"we don't care about disaster relief":

> "Can't verify EIN" · "Needs State Tax Exempt" · "Form isn't for 501c3" ·
> "No Pass Through Non-Profits" · "Must know an employee" · "Need 90 days"

Corporate giving officers verify a nonprofit before approving anything, and the
first place they look is the website. Here is what yours currently shows them:

| They check for | Your site has |
| --- | --- |
| Legal name matching the EIN | **Wrong** — footer still says Sylvan Ramble Lights, Inc. |
| State charitable registration | **Missing** — disclosure has no CH number |
| Evidence of program work | **"Our Impact — Coming Soon!"** |
| Financials / Form 990 | **Absent** |
| Board & governance | 4 names, no roles beyond title, **no treasurer** |
| Recent activity | Newest content is a concluded campaign |

A reviewer landing on that page cannot confirm you are what you say you are. At
least four of the recorded rejections — EIN verification, tax-exempt status,
501c3 form eligibility, pass-through concerns — are questions a properly built
**Transparency page** answers before anyone has to ask.

**This is the highest-ROI page on the new site, and it isn't in the current IA.**
Add `/about/transparency`: legal name, EIN, state registration number,
determination letter, Form 990s, board roster with roles, annual impact numbers.

Related: **you currently have no treasurer.** That's a governance gap a diligent
corporate reviewer or grantmaker will notice, and it sits directly upstream of
the "can't verify" rejections. Worth filling before the next sponsorship cycle.

## 2. The gala probably lost money

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Budgeted cost (business plan) | $9,000 |
| Raised (Givebutter) | $7,259 |
| Goal | $50,000 |
| Attendance | 49 supporters |
| **Net, before labor** | **≈ −$1,700** |

Seven courses, five custom videos, an emcee script, a mayor's endorsement, a
venue, and a volunteer corps — to finish **14% to goal and underwater**.

The gala is the most labor-intensive thing the organization does and its worst
performer. Compare:

| Channel | Result | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light show (2015–2023) | ~$50,000 · 20,000+ visitors · **NBC Nightly News** | Volunteer, own property |
| Facebook: Hilton rooms offer | **700 shares**, organic | $0 |
| Beacons of Hope gala 2025 | $7,259 from 49 people | $9,000 |

The pattern is unambiguous: **your winning channels are mass-participation and
concrete-offer-driven. Your losing channel is high-touch and high-production.**

This argues strongly for reviving the light show as "Lanterns of Hope" — not for
nostalgia, but because it is the only thing you've ever done that produced both
money *and* national earned media, at near-zero marginal cost. It also reaches
thousands of people rather than 49.

Not a recommendation to kill the gala. It builds relationships, and the videos it
produced are your best assets. But it should be sized as a **cultivation event
that breaks even**, with fundraising pressure moved to recurring giving.

## 3. The auction destroyed 72% of the value donated to it

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Retail value donated | $11,699.73 |
| Actually realized | $3,319.00 |
| **Realization rate** | **28.4%** |
| Unsold items | 7 ($856 retail) |
| Unique bidders | **22** |
| Top 5 bidders' share | **64%** of auction revenue |

Forty-eight businesses were solicited, tracked, thanked, and given a page on the
website — to convert $11,700 of donated goods into $3,319, split among 22 people.

**The clearest signal in the data:** items with obvious cash-equivalent value
cleared at or above retail, while bundled "experiences" collapsed.

| Beat retail | Collapsed |
| --- | --- |
| Ocean Prime $200 → **$220** | Portrait + Miami hotel $5,000 → **$150** (3%) |
| Publix $100 → **$105** | Let's Roam group of 10 $150 → **$20** (13%) |
| 1905 Restaurants $100 → **$105** | Wine tasting for 20 $600 → **$130** (22%) |
| Brew Bus $80 → **$184** | MOSI 6-month for 5 $125 → **$35** (28%) |

With 22 bidders in the room, nobody needs a wine tasting for 20 people. The
inventory was mismatched to the audience size — a fixable problem, but it means
**stop soliciting large-group experiences for a small gala.**

That $5,000 portrait session selling for $150 also carries a warning for the
website: **don't publish inflated retail values on sponsor pages.** A stated
value the market rejects by 97% invites skepticism about every other number you
publish.

## 4. The fulfilment sheet is stale, not alarming

The auction fulfilment sheet shows 32 items as "Probobly" and 4 as "No" — but
**all items were in fact delivered.** The sheet was never updated after the
event. No action needed.

Worth noting only as a process point: the record of what happened and what
actually happened have drifted apart. That matters for the transparency page,
because impact numbers published on a website need a source you trust a year
later. Whatever system records a future drive, inspection, or hotel placement
should be the same one the site reads from.

## 5. Your own services were market-tested, and the market said no

Two FLP earned-revenue services were listed in the auction:

| Service | Listed | Sold | Realization |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Professional Weather Station + Install | $650 | $280 | 43% |
| Pre-Storm Drone Property Inspection | $250 | $80 | 32% |

Both underperformed gift cards, which cleared ~100%. Against a warm room of
supporters who already believe in the mission.

That's real pricing signal. Either the prices are above what the market bears, or
**the value isn't legible** — nobody in that room knew what a pre-storm drone
inspection gets them. The business plan leans on these as sustaining revenue
(Lantern Network Membership, drone inspections, weather station services), so this
matters.

For the website: these can't be line items on a services list. They need pages
that do the explaining the auction table couldn't — what you get, what it's worth,
what it costs, and **Maria's story**, which is the proof that an inspection report
turns into an SBA loan. That story converts the abstract into arithmetic.

## 6. The gala produced zero Lantern Keepers — because the program doesn't exist

Fifty-nine minutes of programming, Amy's story, a QR code on screen, and a
**$5,000/year donor match** — and the result was **zero monthly donors.**

That is not an audience failure. Look at where the ask had to land:

- **Mid-dinner, after course 5**, competing with food, wine, and conversation
- **Via a QR code**, pointing at — what, exactly? There is no `/lantern-keeper`
  page. There is no page explaining what a Lantern Keeper *is*, what it funds,
  what tiers exist, or that the match doubles it.
- **To a room of 49 people who had already paid twice**, via ticket and auction
- **With no follow-up sequence** afterwards

You cannot convert people into a program that has no home. **"Lantern Keeper"
existed as a spoken sentence and a QR code, not as a thing anyone could go look
at.** Monthly giving almost never converts in-room anyway; it converts on the
second and third touch, from a page that answers "what am I joining?"

Two consequences:

1. **The $5,000 match went entirely unclaimed.** That's free money that expired.
   **OPEN: is that donor still willing?** If so, it's the single strongest launch
   mechanic the new site has — a real deadline and a 2× multiplier.
2. **The one remaining justification for the gala is relationships and the
   videos.** Its fundraising case is now fully closed: $7,259 gross, ~$9,000
   cost, zero recurring donors.

## 6b. The real problem: nothing you build compounds

Zero recurring donors is a symptom. Trace the whole history:

| Asset built | People reached | What remains today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light show, 2015–2023 | **20,000+ visitors** | **2,500-person email list** (see 6c) |
| NBC Nightly News + 9 press hits | National | Not linked anywhere you own |
| Facebook Hilton post | **700 shares** | Rented reach on someone else's platform |
| Beacons of Hope gala | 49 supporters | **0 recurring donors** |
| Sponsor program | 47 donations | Re-solicited from scratch annually, at 8% |
| Jamaica campaign | — | Concluded |

**Nearly every channel resets to zero each year.** Forty-seven businesses said
yes and next year you start the 560-application grind again. A national news
segment aired and it lives on a YouTube link in a Word document.

The one exception is the email list — which is why it matters far more than its
current reputation suggests.

This is the actual thesis for the website project, and it's bigger than a Framer
migration. The site is the **only** asset available that compounds:

- **Content that ranks** — a `/response/milton-2024` page earns search traffic
  every hurricane season, forever. A Facebook post is dead in 48 hours.
- **A transparency page** that pre-clears sponsors, turning that 8% conversion
  into something better without a single extra application.
- **The email list you already own** — 2,500 people, currently treated as a dead
  asset. See 6c.
- **Recurring donors**, the only revenue that doesn't need re-earning annually.

Everything in the build order below serves that one idea: stop renting, start
owning.

## 6c. The 2,500-person list is not the wrong audience. It's been asked the wrong question.

The stated problem: *"most are uninterested in disaster since it was light show."*

That assumes the list is "people who like Christmas lights." Look at who they
actually are:

- **Tampa Bay households** — the show was a South Tampa neighborhood event
- **who were here for Helene and Milton**, two weeks apart, in 2024
- **who already gave money to charity** at the show (~$50,000 of it)
- **who brought their kids**, repeatedly, for years

They are not disaster-relief *sympathizers*. They are **disaster survivors.** A
large share of that list had roof damage, lost power, evacuated, or sat through
both storms. Nobody on it needs to be convinced that hurricanes matter.

### The ask has been backwards

Asking them to *donate to disaster relief* asks them to care about strangers.
Instead, **offer them the service:**

| Instead of | Offer |
| --- | --- |
| "Support families affected by hurricanes" | "Free roof inspection if Milton damaged your home" |
| "Donate to our disaster fund" | "Pre-storm drone inspection — documentation before you need it" |
| "Help us prepare communities" | "We'll install a weather station on your street" |
| "Give monthly" | "Hurricane prep checklist for Tampa Bay homeowners" |

Every one of those is something a Tampa homeowner wants **for themselves**. And
they map exactly onto the earned-revenue lines in the business plan — drone
inspections, weather stations, readiness kits, Lantern Keeper. The list isn't
just a donor base; it is **the market for the paid services**, which the auction
showed are currently priced into a vacuum.

### And they are the launch audience for the light show's return

"Lanterns of Hope" is the one channel with a proven track record. This list is
2,500 pre-qualified attendees of the exact thing being revived. Sponsored
lanterns at $25, at a 10% response, is ~$6,250 — comparable to the entire 2025
gala, at almost no cost.

Even cold, at a 2% response, 2,500 people yields ~50. **The gala produced 49.**

### Caution: do not blast it

If the list hasn't been mailed in 1–2+ years, a mass send will bounce hard, hit
spam traps, and can get `floridalanternproject.org` flagged — which would damage
donation receipts and every other transactional email. Re-engage properly:

1. **Verify** the list (Kickbox, ZeroBounce) and drop the dead addresses
2. **Warm up** — small batches to the most recently engaged first
3. **Lead with the light show**, not with an ask. "Remember this? Here's what
   happened next, and here's what's coming back."
4. **Let them self-select** — separate "tell me about the light show" from
   "I'm a Tampa homeowner, tell me about storm prep"
5. **Sunset** non-openers rather than mailing them forever

**OPEN:** which provider holds the list, and when was it last mailed? Both answers
change the sequence.

## 7. What this means for the build

Reordered by expected return:

1. **`/about/transparency`** — unblocks sponsorship conversions. Highest ROI page.
2. **`/give/lantern-keeper`** — build the program that produced zero donors
   because it had nowhere to live. Needs: what it is, tiers, what each funds,
   the match (if still live), and a real recurring checkout.
3. **Email capture on every page** — the current newsletter box needs a real
   provider behind it. Without a list, the next 20,000 visitors vanish too.
4. **`/response/*`** — Helene and Milton written up permanently. This is what
   grantmakers ask for, and the "one major grant" goal depends on it.
5. **Service pages carrying Maria's and Peter's stories** — make the earned-
   revenue offers legible enough to price correctly.
6. **`/events/lanterns-of-hope`** — the light show is the proven channel, and
   this time it should capture emails at the door.
7. Sponsor pages, blog, everything else.

Note that the current site's ordering is almost exactly inverted: ~96 of its 109
pages are sponsor detail pages.
