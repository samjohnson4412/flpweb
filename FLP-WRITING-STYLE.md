# FLP writing style: emails, posts, scripts and playbooks

Sam approved this style on October 9, 2026, in the final Hurricane Isaias playbook (`review/FLP-isaias-playbook-2026-10-09.md`). Use it for every outward-facing message: partner and agency emails, list emails, social posts, phone scripts, voicemail greetings and report-backs. `FLP-FACTS.md` still governs every fact and content rule.

## Voice

- **Calm, measured and professional.** Write like a careful nonprofit, not a startup or an activist. Warm, but never breezy or clever.
- **Hedge what you can't guarantee.** Say "may be able to help," "may be available to eligible homeowners and renters," "once conditions are safe to fly," and "we'll see whether we can help identify an option." Never promise outcomes, timing ("we'll call you back today") or availability.
- **No digs and no drama.** Don't imply officials, insurers or other organizations fail people. Drop lines like "most people are never told," "a real person," "a stranger," "nobody up there has heard of us," "one forward from you does more than anything we could post."
- **Plain, descriptive subject lines.** "Free post-storm help for families you serve," "An update on our Hurricane Isaias response." No teasers and no "what we didn't do."
- **Full sentences, Oxford comma** ("FEMA, SBA, and local assistance programs"). Contractions are fine.

## Standard phrasing

- **Storm line:** always "non-emergency storm line" or "non-emergency storm questions by phone at (813) 733-8806."
- **911 line:** "If you are in immediate danger, call 911." Add "We are not an emergency service" where it fits.
- **Deployment:** "We are coordinating with local organizations and will only travel into affected areas when requested and when conditions are safe." Or "We are coordinating from Tampa rather than deploying on our own."
- **Drone work:** "Drone documentation of roof and property damage, with a written report," flown "under FAA Part 107" by "an FAA Part 107 certified remote pilot." Never "inspection" until the license is issued.
- **Aid help:** "Help navigating FEMA, SBA, and local assistance programs." "SBA disaster loans may be available to eligible homeowners and renters as well as businesses."
- **2024 record:** "assisted more than 50 individuals and families."
- **Service list:** the same three bullets everywhere: drone documentation, FEMA/SBA/local aid help, and the non-emergency storm line.
- **Call to action:** the phone number plus a site link (floridalanternproject.org/help/request or the storm page).
- **Evacuation:** "If local officials tell you to evacuate, please follow their instructions." Then offer help "if pets or transportation are making it difficult for you to leave."
- **Damage advice:** neutral record-keeping: photos and video before cleanup "when possible," receipts, claim numbers, "reports, estimates, photographs, and other documents."

## Structure

- **Playbooks:** title, byline and date line, a one-line stance, then "Where things stand," "Ground rules," "The plan, in order" (dated checklists), scripts, each email with sending notes, social posts (Facebook full text, Instagram short caption), the intake list, then sources.
- **Placeholders:** square brackets, with instructions inside when needed: "[Delete any line that does not apply.]" or "[ONLY IF REGISTRATION IS CURRENT: …]".
- **Report-backs:** bullet the true numbers, add a bracketed fallback line if FLP didn't deploy, then one short permitted story. Frame lessons as forward-looking ("We're working on that by building relationships with…"), never as failure.
- **Signatures:** partner and agency emails use "Sam Johnson / President, Florida Lantern Project, Inc. · EIN 86-3574323 / (813) 733-8806 · info@floridalanternproject.org · floridalanternproject.org." List emails use "Sam Johnson / Florida Lantern Project."
