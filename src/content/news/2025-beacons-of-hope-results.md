On 7 June we held Beacons of Hope, our first gala: seven courses tracing Florida's
history, told alongside the stories of families we helped through Hurricanes
Helene and Milton.

**The result: $7,259 from 49 supporters.** Our goal was $50,000.

We are publishing that number because a charity that only reports its wins is
teaching you not to trust its numbers. Here is the fuller picture.

## What worked

Guests heard directly from the people this work is for. Peter, who evacuated a
dorm with a cat no shelter would take. Maria, whose free roof inspection led her
to an SBA loan she didn't know existed. Amy, hit by two hurricanes in nine days
with two children and three animals. Mayor Jane Castor recorded a message
recognizing grassroots disaster response.

Forty-six Florida businesses donated to the silent auction. That generosity is
real and we are grateful for it.

## What didn't

The auction converted poorly. Goods with a stated retail value of roughly $11,700
realized about $3,300 across 22 bidders. Items sized for large groups — a wine
tasting for twenty, a scavenger hunt for ten — went for a fraction of their value
in a room of fifty people. That's our planning error, not our donors'.

We also asked guests to become monthly donors and **nobody did.** We think we
know why: there was nowhere for them to go. We described a monthly giving program
from a stage and pointed a QR code at a generic donation form. There was no page
explaining what joining actually meant.

That page exists now. It's called [Lantern Keeper](/give/lantern-keeper).

## What happens next

A gala is a good way to bring people together and an expensive way to raise
money. We'll keep holding one, sized honestly as a way to say thank you — and
put our fundraising weight behind monthly giving and the return of the light
show, which is the only thing we've ever done that reached twenty thousand people.
