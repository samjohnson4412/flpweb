## Ground rules

Every email, post and call this week follows these six rules.

1. **No home inspector license claims until the state issues it.** Say "FAA Part 107 certified drone pilot." Call the work "drone damage documentation" or "a drone roof survey with a written report," not a home inspection.
2. **No donation ask until the Florida charitable registration is renewed.** It lapsed and the renewal is in progress. Soliciting while unregistered breaks Chapter 496, so no "donate" posts and no Lantern Keeper ask in Email 3 until it's done.
3. **Coordinate, don't self-deploy.** Stay reachable by phone, work through county emergency management, 211 and the organizations already serving the area, and go up only if one of them asks for something we can do. If we go, we go self-sufficient and check in with the county emergency operations center (EOC) on arrival.
4. **Promise only what we can deliver.** A person on the phone, help finding FEMA, SBA and local aid, and drone documentation once it's safe to fly. Phone and data plans through partner organizations. No lodging, cash or supplies.
5. **911 first, always.** We are not an emergency service.
6. **No content-gathering.** No photos of people or homes without permission, no children without a parent's consent, first names only with permission.

## Phone script: emergency management, 211, COAD

Keep it under 30 seconds; they are busy. The point of the call is a name.

> "Hi, this is Sam Johnson with the Florida Lantern Project, a Florida 501(c)(3) based in Tampa.
>
> We're reachable by phone, and we'd rather coordinate through you than show up unannounced. I'm an FAA Part 107 certified drone pilot, and after it passes I can document roof and property damage with a written report families can use for insurance and SBA. We also run a phone line a person answers, and we help people through FEMA and SBA paperwork.
>
> Who should I talk to about where we'd be useful, and who handles drone flights under the temporary flight restriction?"

Then stop talking and write down the name, number and anything they ask for.

## Storm line intake

Ask these in order on every call and log the answers in the sheet. The list becomes the recovery route and the numbers for Email 3.

1. "Are you safe right now?" If not: "Please hang up and call 911." Stay on until they agree.
2. "What's your name and the best number to reach you?"
3. "Where are you? Address or nearest cross streets, and county."
4. "Who's with you, people and animals?"
5. "What's the one thing you most need help with?"
6. "Do you have a working phone and data?"
7. "Do you own or rent? Do you have homeowners or flood insurance?"
8. "Can I call you back in a few days to check on you?" Never skip this one.
